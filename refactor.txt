To simplify, these 6 conditions are the "Rules of the Road" for finding where a specific temperature layer (a Theta level, e.g., 300K) sits in the sky. Because the ground is not flat and the air temperature changes, the 300K layer might be high up in one city but "hit the ground" in another. Your Fortran code uses these conditions to decide how to handle those different situations: 1. The "Underground" Case 1) Theta level is below surface level: The target temperature (300K) is colder than the air at the ground. Physically, this means the 300K surface doesn't exist in the air here; it is effectively "underground."Action: The code usually "parks" this level at the ground pressure (\(P_{sfc}\)).3) Theta level is at the surface: The target temperature exactly matches the ground temperature.Action: No interpolation needed; just use the surface pressure and winds. 2. The "Boundary Layer" Case 4) Theta level is between surface and 1000 mb level: The target is very close to the ground, between the earth and the first standard pressure level (usually 1000 hPa).5) Theta level is between surface and other mandatory layers: The target is between the ground and a higher level (like 850 hPa or 700 hPa).Action for 4 & 5: These use the Surface Injection. Instead of interpolating between two "sky" levels, the code interpolates between the Ground and the nearest Sky level. 3. The "Pure Atmosphere" Case 6) Theta is between 2 mandatory layers: The target is floating high in the sky between two standard levels (e.g., between 500 hPa and 400 hPa).Action: This is standard interpolation using only model data.2) Theta level is above pressure level: The target temperature is so hot (or cold, depending on direction) that it is higher than the very top of your data.Action: The code assigns a "missing" value or caps it at the highest available level. Why this is a "Lengthy" headache: In a modern Python script, you would just use one line like np.interp.
